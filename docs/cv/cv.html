Research

I am a plant ecologist working on global-change impacts on biodiversity and ecosystem functioning, mainly in alpine and arctic ecosystems. To get at the processes behind plant population, community, and ecosystem responses, and at how those responses depend on context, I replicate experiments along broad environmental gradients. I use trait-based approaches, including intraspecific trait variation, and I use synthesis (meta-analysis and systematic reviews) to look for mechanisms and broad-scale patterns. I am also interested in conservation and ecosystem ecology, including grazing as a way to manage ecosystems under global change. This work has included contributing to the IPBES assessment of invasive alien species, and to the ecological condition assessments of forest and alpine ecosystems in Norway.

I work in international teams, raise external funding, and teach and supervise students from BSc to PhD. I use R and Git for large biodiversity and climate datasets, and I am responsible for data management and reproducible workflows in large projects, including the use of those data in earth system models. I coordinate methods work for the community, including the R package traitstrap and the ClimEx handbook of standardised field and laboratory methods for terrestrial climate-change experiments (114 authors, 62 protocols). I also do educational research on open and reproducible science.
